# MAGASIN — MASTER OBJECTIVE

**Status:** PROPOSED / FOUNDATION
**Version:** 0.1
**Purpose:** The permanent north star for all future MAGASIN planning, operations, data, SOP, software, automation and AI work.

## 1. Ultimate objective

Build MAGASIN into a **profitable, scalable, self-operating multi-store beverage business** whose operations do not depend on the Owner personally handling every daily task.

The company must be able to:

1. Operate each store consistently through clear standards, roles and SOPs.
2. Coordinate people, products, cash, inventory, sales and customer experience through a common operating model.
3. Measure performance at store, channel, product, labor and company level.
4. Detect deviations, shortages, waste, weak sales, excessive labor cost and other exceptions early.
5. Turn data into recommended actions, while keeping important decisions under defined human authority.
6. Replicate the operating model when opening new stores, without rebuilding the company from scratch.
7. Improve continuously through a closed loop of **standard → execution → measurement → exception → action → review → improvement**.
8. Use the webapp as the digital execution/control layer of the business, not as the business model itself.

## 2. What MAGASIN is building

MAGASIN is not merely building a workforce application, POS companion or employee portal.

MAGASIN is building a **Business Operating System (BOS)** composed of:

- Business strategy and economic model
- Customer and commercial system
- Store operating system
- People and organization system
- Supply chain and inventory system
- Finance and unit-economics system
- Performance and governance system
- Data architecture
- Digital applications and integrations
- Automation and AI decision-support

## 3. Non-negotiable principle

Technology follows the operating model.

The order is:

**Business truth → operating rules → process → data → controls → software → automation → AI**.

Software must not invent business rules that have not been deliberately defined and approved.

## 4. Definition of 'self-operating'

A self-operating MAGASIN is not one where software does everything automatically.

It is one where:

- recurring work is standardized;
- responsibility is assigned to the correct role;
- routine decisions are automated where safe;
- complex decisions are assisted by data and recommendations;
- approvals and financial/control decisions remain with authorized humans;
- exceptions are surfaced automatically;
- management can understand the state of the business without being physically present at every store.

## 5. Profitability as a system constraint

Growth is not the primary success metric by itself.

MAGASIN must pursue **profitable growth**. Every store, product, promotion, labor decision, supply-chain decision and expansion decision should ultimately be connected to unit economics and company cash generation.

Key management questions must include:

- How much revenue is generated?
- What is the contribution after product costs?
- What is labor costing relative to demand and sales?
- What is the real store operating contribution?
- Where is waste occurring?
- Which products, channels, stores and promotions create or destroy value?
- What operational change can improve profit without damaging customer experience or brand standards?

Industry data reinforces this priority: food and labor are among the most material restaurant cost categories, and profitable limited-service operators in the National Restaurant Association's 2025 benchmark showed lower median labor cost ratios than loss-making operators. These figures are management benchmarks, not MAGASIN targets. (National Restaurant Association, 2025.)

## 6. Scale principle

MAGASIN must be designed so that the system works at:

**1 store → 4 stores → 10 stores → 20 stores → 50+ stores**

without requiring the Owner to manually reproduce every operational decision.

## 7. Decision hierarchy

The future operating model should distinguish four execution modes:

### AUTOMATIC
The system executes a predefined, low-risk rule.

### ASSISTED
The system analyzes data and proposes an action; an authorized person decides.

### CONTROLLED
A human decision is required and the system enforces permissions, evidence and auditability.

### MANUAL
The activity requires human judgment and cannot safely be reduced to a deterministic or assisted workflow.

## 8. Permanent test for every future feature

Before adding a feature to MAGASIN, ask:

1. What real business problem does this solve?
2. Which operating rule does it implement?
3. Who owns the outcome?
4. What data does it require?
5. What KPI or control verifies success?
6. What happens when reality deviates from the normal case?
7. Can the capability scale to more stores and staff?
8. Does it improve revenue, margin, productivity, quality, control, customer experience or management capacity?

If these questions cannot be answered, the feature is not ready to become a production rule.

## 9. Reference operating loop

**PLAN → EXECUTE → MEASURE → DETECT → DECIDE → ACT → VERIFY → LEARN → STANDARDIZE**

Every major MAGASIN operating domain should eventually implement this loop.

## 10. Master statement

> **MAGASIN's ultimate goal is to build a profitable, scalable and increasingly self-operating multi-store beverage company in which people, processes, money, products, customers, data and technology are governed as one integrated Business Operating System; the webapp is the digital execution, visibility, control and automation layer that enables that system to run, scale and improve without the Owner being the bottleneck.**
